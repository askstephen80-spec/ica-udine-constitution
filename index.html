    Chapter One Isi nke mbụ

    Who we are

    A family of Igbo sons and daughters living in Italy, organised to keep the culture alive abroad and to look after one another.

    Preface and Articles 1 to 6

    SummaryFull text

    We, the sons and daughters of Igbo indigenes of Nigeria living in Italy, do hereby, firmly and solemnly, resolve to live and work in harmony and concord as one indivisible family.

    From the Preface. Constituted on 16 July 2000.

    What the association is

    Article 5 fixes the character of the association. Article 1 adds the values the constitution protects: freedom, equality, justice, fair play and democracy.

    Non-profit

    Money raised serves members and the culture. Art. 5A

    Non-political, non-religious

    No party line and no church line. Art. 5B

    Democratic and cultural

    Decisions by vote; purpose rooted in Igbo tradition. Art. 5C

    Where it reaches

    • Udine is the administrative headquarters. Art. 4
    • The association covers every city in the province of Udine, and can take in members from beyond it in some cases. Art. 4
    • It keeps contact with sister associations and cultures in Italy. Art. 4
    • The current logo shows the red cap between two ivory tusks, ringed by the words Igbo Cultural Association, Udine (FVG) Italy, with the motto Unity, Peace & Progress. The 2016 text describes an earlier version with the motto “Igbo Bu Eze”. Art. 3

    Ten aims

    Lettered A to J as in Article 6.

    1. APromote peace, unity, understanding and friendship among all members.
    2. BProject the Igbo heritage of oneness, cooperation, self-discipline, self-determination and hospitality.
    3. CContribute to the development of the Igbos and of Nigeria through socio-cultural and political dialogue.
    4. DKeep ourselves informed through prompt sharing of information and positive education.
    5. EUnite all sons and daughters resident in the province of Udine, and in Italy generally.
    6. FPromote the socio-cultural identity of the Igbo people and Igbo culture as a whole.
    7. GImprove members' socio-economic well-being through close relationships and cooperation.
    8. HStrengthen members' position through patriotism and help in times of need.
    9. IHelp Igbo parents teach their children our culture and tradition.
    10. JCultivate discipline and respect for one another, whatever a person's social or economic status.

        Chapter Three Isi nke atọ

        Elections

        A general election every three years in December, an open ballot, and a new executive in office each January.

        Article 11

        SummaryFull text

        The election cycle

        The whole process may take no more than three months, September to December, so the administration can hand over in January. A committee appointed for the purpose runs it and is dissolved afterwards. Art. 11.8–10

        September to November

        The cycle may open in September. An electoral committee is appointed and given full access to the financial records; candidates with arrears clear them by paying the committee, which reports to the assembly. 11.10, 11.17–18

        December

        General election by open ballot. The cycle must close this month. 11.8, 11.13

        January

        Handover to the new executive. 11.9

        No more than three months in allRepeats every three years

        Who can stand

        Every condition must hold. Missing one disqualifies the candidate automatically.

        • At least 12 months a member; 24 months to run for President.11.11
        • Financially up to date in every account to the last general meeting before the election. Arrears can be cleared by paying the electoral committee.11.16–17
        • A valid residence permit, and a good, active member.11.19
        • Living in the province of Udine, to be reachable in an emergency.11.20
        • Not a member of the electoral commission.11.15
        • No other member of the same family, such as a spouse or child, on the executive.11.25

        Members of either sex may stand for any elective office on equal terms (11.4). The office of President is reserved for a male member by Igbo tradition, because of the traditional rites the President performs (11.5).

        The President's terms

        Other officers may be re-elected without limit. The President may serve two consecutive terms, then must sit out three years. Art. 11.1–3

        Term 13 years
        Term 23 years
        Wait3 years
        Eligibleagain

        During the wait, a former President may stand for any other office.

        Leaving office early

        • Resign by letter to the General Assembly.11.21–22
        • Removed by a vote of no confidence when found wanting in character or otherwise.11.23
        • By-election to fill the seat, decided by two-thirds of members present that day.11.24

        Who votes, and who elects whom

        General election

        Only financial members vote, by open ballot. Offices 1 to 14 of Article 10 are elected. 11.6, 11.13–14

        Legislative votes

        All financial members may vote on laws, whatever the state of their account. 11.12

        Inside each body

        Elders elect their Chairman internally; youths elect the Youth Leader; women elect the Women Leader. 10.13–14, 11.7

          Chapter Four Isi nke anọ

          Law, money and discipline

          How laws are made and blocked, how the four bodies relate, where the money goes, and what each breach of conduct costs.

          Articles 12 to 14

          SummaryFull text

          Equal before the law

          All members have equal social dignity and are equal before the law, without distinction of sex, race, language, religion, political views or personal and social condition. Art. 12.1

          Making, blocking and changing laws

          Two thresholds do almost all the work. Votes are counted among the members present at a regular meeting called by the President.

          More than halfTwo-thirds

          Simple majority

          • Major legislation at a regular meeting12.8
          • A tie is broken by the Vice President's vote12.9

          Two-thirds majority

          • A law proposed by the Executives12.10
          • Overturning a presidential veto12.13
          • A by-election11.24
          • Amending the constitution, second vote19.2

          Three months to take effect

          A new or revised law starts at least three months after it passes, unless the house overrules this for an urgent reason. 12.11

          The veto must be explained

          The President may veto a decision he judges harmful, and must explain to the house in detail why. 12.12

          Cases not covered

          Where the constitution is silent, the meeting may take disciplinary or legal measures at the President's discretion. 14.16

          The Council of Elders

          The judicial arm of the association. Membership is decided by the incumbent President. Art. 10.1F, 12.4–7

          Judges

          Resolves disputes between members in special cases, with both parties present on equal terms and within a reasonable time.

          Stabilises

          Acts as temporary government if the executive is vacant, until a new election is held.

          Guides

          Helps the leadership plan and carry out programmes, especially cultural ones.

          Directs rites

          Its Chairman directs when and how traditional programmes are handled at social events.

          The four bodies and their money

          The Executives, the Council of Elders, the Youth Wing and the Women Action Group are semi-autonomous. They have their own statutes and resources, follow this constitution, need the general house's consent for any major function, and may not register separately with the Comune. Art. 12.14–19, 12.22

          Money raised at a joint event

          75% stays in the general account
          25% back

          All money raised is handed to the general account immediately and reported at the next meeting; then a quarter goes back to the body that took part. The Assembly is not obliged to fund the bodies, but may if it helps the association's image. 12.20–21

            Chapter Five Isi nke ise

            Culture and activities

            The reason the association exists: to practise Igbo tradition and pass it to our children so it stays alive in the next generation.

            Article 15

            SummaryFull text

            The association's year

            Fixed events from Articles 9, 11, 13 and 15 on one ring.

            • Children's Day, 27 May. Kept for the children, who are the main reason the association was formed, so they grow up interested in what the Igbos of Udine do. 15.7
            • New Yam festival, August to September, at the start of the harvest season. The Mmau festival is also held in the harvest season. 15.5–6
            • End-of-year party. All members of every category welcome the new year, and the President gives the state of the union address. 15.10
            • Election cycle, September to December, every third year, with the general election in December. 11.8–9
            • Handover in January, when the new executive and the fiscal year begin. 11.9, 13A
            • General meeting on the second Sunday of every month. 9F

            Showing up for one another

            When the association holds a cultural festival, or a financial member has a wedding, birthday or funeral, every member is obliged to attend. Art. 15.1–3

            Skipping a member's occasion

            €20

            Fine for not attending a festival or a financial member's wedding, birthday, funeral or similar.

            Formal invitation from a recognised group

            €50

            Clubs, unions and associations that invite us formally with a hot drink receive €50 from the association, and we attend.

            Private invitation from anyone else

            No money

            Not valid for a financial contribution, but members are encouraged to attend in large numbers.

            The kolanut

            Whenever Igbo people gather for a social activity, the host presents kolanut, ọjị. Art. 15.4

            1

            The host presents the kolanut to the gathering.

            2

            By culture, the presiding Chairman or President has the right to break it.

            3

            Or he passes it to whomever he chooses, usually the oldest person present, who then keeps the same right.

            Honours

            Merit award

            Given from time to time to members, and even non-members, for positive contributions to society. It is a boldly printed certificate in a large photo frame, never money. A committee vets each candidate. Recipients pay nothing, though a gift to the association is welcome. 15.8

            Chieftaincy title

            The association may confer a title on good citizens of our Igbo community in Udine. A committee checks that the person is legally, religiously, socially, culturally, economically and productively active. Anyone who claims a native title on their own must go through the same screening and pay: 15.9

            €500member
            €1,000non-member

            A certificate is then issued for the record.

                Chapter Seven Isi nke asaa

                Epilogue

                Audits at the end of every tenure, service without pay, and the procedure for changing this constitution.

                Articles 17 to 19

                SummaryFull text

                Auditors

                At the end of every tenure the meeting appoints a committee of auditors to examine the association's finances and present an accurate picture. The association sets their deadline according to the volume of work. No outgoing officer may sit on it, and its work ends when the report is presented. Art. 17

                Service is unpaid

                “Per la patria è la gloria.”

                No officer or member of any category receives monthly pay or other reward for serving. Expenses incurred while helping the association are repaid if the member asks and the association certifies them. Art. 18

                Changing the constitution

                It can be amended in part or as a whole when the association needs it. Art. 19

                1

                The house deliberates on the change a first time.

                2

                A second deliberation follows; both fall within three months.

                3

                The second vote must reach a two-thirds majority.

                4

                The change is written into the minutes and noted by the President until the whole text is next reviewed.

                The constitution must be faithfully observed as the fundamental law of the association by all members and dependent bodies. 19.4

                  About this site

                  This site presents the constitution of the Igbo Cultural Association Udine as printed in the revised edition of January 2016.

                  Each chapter has a plain-language summary and the full text. The summaries point to the article they come from, so you can always check the exact wording.

                  The full text follows the printed booklet. Obvious spelling slips are corrected; the wording is otherwise unchanged.

                  Amendments passed since January 2016 are recorded in the minutes books and take precedence over this text (Art. 19.3). One correction is visible in the scanned booklet: beside the registration total of €45 a handwritten note reads €50 (Art. 8B).

                  Two details on this site are newer than the printed text: the current logo (Art. 3) and the meeting venue, now Via Lumignacco 30, 33100 Udine (Art. 9E). The full text keeps the 2016 wording and notes the change beside it.
